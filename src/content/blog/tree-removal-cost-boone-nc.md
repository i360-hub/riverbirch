The first question on nearly every call I get is the same: "What will this cost me?" It's a fair question, and you deserve a straight answer. Here's the honest version from someone who prices tree work in the High Country every week: nobody can give you a real number without seeing the tree — but I can show you exactly what sets the price, so you can look at your own tree and know roughly where it lands.

> Most single-tree removals around Boone, Blowing Rock, and Banner Elk land roughly between $500 and $2,500. Small, easy-access trees run less; big trees close to a house — the kind that have to come down in roped sections — run more. The only number that counts is a written on-site estimate, which is why ours are free.

## Size drives the price more than anything

Height and trunk diameter are the biggest factors, plain and simple. A 25-foot ornamental in an open yard in Vilas is a quick job. A 70-foot white pine leaning over a roof in Blowing Rock is a full-crew day with climbing, rigging, and a lot of brush to chip. More wood, more time, more risk — that's the math behind every quote.

## In the mountains, access matters as much as the tree

This is the part that surprises flatland transplants. Two identical trees can be completely different jobs depending on the lot. A pine in an open pasture outside West Jefferson is a simple fell. The same pine on a 30-degree slope in Seven Devils, with no room to get a truck within a hundred yards, has to come down piece by piece — climbers, ropes, every limb lowered by hand. Steep ground, soft soil after rain, long carries for brush: that's High Country tree work, and it's priced accordingly.

Things that push a quote up:

- The tree hangs over a house, deck, garage, fence, or power line
- No equipment access — the whole job is done by climbing
- Steep slopes or soft, wet ground where trucks can't set up
- Tight mountain driveways with no turnaround for the chipper

## Species matters here too

Our forests aren't like the Piedmont's. White pines grow tall and fast and are usually the most straightforward removals. Mature red and white oaks have wide, heavy canopies — more wood, more rigging, bigger job at the same height. And dead trees cost more whatever the species: dead wood is brittle and unpredictable, which makes the work slower and more dangerous for the crew. If you have a hemlock dying back from woolly adelgid — and there are plenty in Watauga and Ashe Counties — don't wait. It only gets more expensive and more hazardous as it declines.

## Storm and emergency work is priced differently

A tree through your roof at midnight is not a scheduled job. Emergency and storm-damage work runs above normal rates because the crew is working around the clock, often in bad weather, on trees that are already broken and loaded with tension. One tip: if a storm-damaged tree is on a structure, take photos before anyone touches it — your insurance company will want them. We handle [storm and ice damage cleanup](/storm-damage-tree-removal) across the High Country, with priority scheduling during our operating hours.

## Don't forget the stump

Most removal quotes don't include the stump unless you ask. [Stump grinding](/stump-grinding) is usually priced by stump diameter, and adding it while we're already on site is cheaper than a return trip. Ask for it in the same estimate.

## How to keep the cost down

**Bundle your trees.** Three removals in one visit cost less than three separate trips — the crew and equipment are already there.

**Don't wait on a dying tree.** A declining tree gets pricier to remove every year. Once it's fully dead and brittle, the price goes up with the risk.

**Schedule in the slower season.** Late fall and winter are quieter, which can mean more flexibility in the schedule.

**Get more than one quote.** I'll say it even though I'm one of the quotes. Just make sure everyone bidding is insured — a cheap quote from an uninsured crew isn't cheap if something goes wrong on your property. We're fully insured, and our estimates are free, on-site, and in writing.
